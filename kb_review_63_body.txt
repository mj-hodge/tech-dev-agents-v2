### Code Review

**Findings:**

- **[MEDIUM]** SPS normalization has a catalog-size bias: max_possible = len(brand_asins) * tier_weights penalizes brands with large catalogs vs. small concentrated ones. A brand with 200 ASINs where 50 rank in the top 1,000 will score lower than a brand with 5 ASINs where 2 are top-100, even if the first brand dominates sales volume. Consider percentile-based tiers relative to category size in the implementation phase.
- **[MEDIUM]** brand_impressions is initialized to 0 in Step 1-2 pseudocode but never incremented - only brand_clicks and brand_purchases are summed. SSS formula currently only uses purchase share so this does not break the model, but impression share is silently missing if needed downstream.
- **[LOW]** SSS weighted-average formula computes a weighted average of purchase-share ratios, not the brand share of total category purchase volume. Both approaches are defensible - article should note the distinction for implementers.
- **[LOW]** Branded query multiplier (0.3) is an untested constant. For brands with strong branded search, this could systematically deflate SSS. Should be flagged as a calibration parameter during validation, not a settled constant.
- **[LOW]** BSR tier thresholds (top-100, top-1,000, etc.) not normalized to category size. Top-100 = top 1% in a 10,000-ASIN category but top 50% in a 200-ASIN subcategory. Consider percentile-based tiers in implementation.
- **[LOW]** Reference 14 (Karpathy LLM wiki gist) has no connection to Amazon brand category ranking - appears to be a stray reference from a prior article. Remove in a follow-up commit.
- **[INFO]** Temporal half-life values (7-day BFS, 3-day SSS) lack empirical backing - should be flagged as calibration parameters.
